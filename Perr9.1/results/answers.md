  - Manufacturer (and authorised representative, if applicable) identification and SRN (if available).

Documents to have ready (Class IIa, sterile)

Technical documentation (MDR Annex II and III)
- Device description, intended purpose, classification rationale (Annex II, 1–2; Article 51)
- GSPR checklist with objective evidence, especially Infection/microbial contamination and packaging (Annex I, Section 11.2–11.7; 23.3)
- Risk management file, including sterilization- and packaging-related hazards and controls (ISO 14971; MDR Annex I, 3)
- Design and manufacturing information, including sterilization process flow, equipment, environments, and controls (Annex II, 3)
- Verification and validation evidence:
  - Sterilization validation (protocols, IQ/OQ/PQ, routine control plans, requalification schedule) appropriate to method:
    - EO: EN ISO 11135; bioburden/sterility per EN ISO 11737-1/-2; EO residuals per EN ISO 10993-7
    - Radiation: EN ISO 11137-1/-2/-3; bioburden/sterility per EN ISO 11737-1/-2
    - Steam: EN ISO 17665-1
    - Aseptic processing (if applicable): EN ISO 13408 series; cleanroom per EN ISO 14644-1/-2
  - Sterile barrier system and packaging validation: EN ISO 11607-1/-2; seal process IQ/OQ/PQ; integrity and seal strength tests (e.g., ASTM F1929/F2096/F88); distribution simulation and transportation; shelf-life (real-time/accelerated aging, ASTM F1980) with post-sterilization/package integrity/functionality
  - Biocompatibility considering post-sterilization condition (EN ISO 10993 series) and material stability after sterilization/aging
  - Product performance/function after worst-case sterilization and end-of-shelf-life
- Labeling/IFU with sterile status, method, expiry date, single-use/re-sterilization restrictions, storage, and handling (Annex I, 23.3; Annex VI UDI)
- Clinical evaluation and PMCF (Annex XIV), if relevant to safety after sterilization/shelf-life
- PMS plan and PSUR (Class IIa: at least every 2 years) (Annex III; Article 86); vigilance/trend procedures (Articles 87–89)

Notified Body and conformity assessment
- Valid NB certificate and scope covering “aspects relating to establishing, securing and maintaining sterility” (Article 52; Annex IX, 2.3; or Annex XI)
- NB-reviewed representative technical documentation where applicable (Annex IX, 2.3/3)

QMS procedures and records (ISO 13485:2016)
- Process validation and change control for sterilization and packaging (7.5.6; 7.1; 7.3.9)
- Particular requirements for sterile medical devices: approved sterilization parameters, release by authorized person, batch records, requalification (7.5.5)
- Cleanliness/contamination control and environmental monitoring (6.4; EN ISO 14644-1/-2 as applicable)
- Supplier qualification and quality agreements for external sterilization/packaging (7.4.1–7.4.3), including review/approval of their validations where used
- Device batch history records including sterilization load records, BI/parametric data, acceptance against defined criteria, and lot release (4.2.4; 7.5.1; 7.5.5)
- Calibration/maintenance of relevant equipment and monitoring devices (7.6)
- Internal audits, training, CAPA related to sterilization/packaging processes (8.2–8.5)

Likely gaps if you have no sterilization validation protocol
- Nonconformity to MDR GSPRs for sterile devices: cannot demonstrate sterility assurance or validated method (Annex I, Section 11.2–11.7), and cannot justify shelf-life or sterile barrier integrity (Annex I, 11.6; 23.3)
- Technical documentation deficiency: Annex II, Section 4 requires verification/validation evidence including sterilization and packaging validation; absence of an approved protocol typically implies missing IQ/OQ/PQ, routine control plans, and requalification criteria
- QMS nonconformity: lack of pre-approved protocol violates ISO 13485 process validation controls (7.5.6) and particular requirements for sterile devices (7.5.5), including defined acceptance criteria and documented release conditions
- NB conformity assessment risk: For Class IIa sterile, NB must assess sterility-related aspects (Article 52; Annex IX, 2.3). Without a protocol (and completed validation), NB scope may be unsupported; major NC likely
- Downstream evidence gaps commonly co-exist:
  - Missing or incomplete bioburden trend data and sterility testing strategy (EN ISO 11737-1/-2)
  - No EO residuals validation (EN ISO 10993-7) if EO is used
  - Inadequate packaging process validation and sterile barrier performance over shelf-life and transport (EN ISO 11607-1/-2)
  - Lack of Worst-Case definition and family grouping rationale for sterilization and packaging validations
  - No requalification/ongoing control plan (dose audits for radiation per EN ISO 11137-2; annual requalification for EO/steam per EN ISO 11135/17665; BI/parametric release criteria)
  - Incomplete DHRs for sterilization lots (missing load configuration, cycle data, BI results/parametric data, approvals)
  - IFU/label claims of sterility/expiry not supported by validation (Annex I, 23.3)
  - Risk management file not linking sterilization/packaging hazards to controls and residual risks (ISO 14971; Annex I, 3)

Immediate remediation (pragmatic for audit)
- Perform and approve a gap assessment mapping current evidence to Annex I Section 11 and EN ISO 1113x/17665/11607 requirements
- Generate and approve sterilization validation protocol(s) with defined acceptance criteria and worst-case rationale; plan IQ/OQ/PQ and requalification
- If time does not allow completion, prepare a documented, risk-based interim control and CAPA plan with firm timelines; review market status and consider stopping release of “sterile” product until validation evidence exists
